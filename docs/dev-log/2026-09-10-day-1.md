# Day 1 — Why I'm building a live map of human curiosity

**2026-09-10**

The best conversations I've ever had about a book started with a stranger noticing
I was reading the same thing. Not a follower count. A coincidence-of-curiosity.

CurioMap is an attempt to make that happen at map scale: a global, open-source,
geolocation-based **reading graph** where *what you're curious about right now* is the
signal — and who you are, where you live (precisely), and who follows you are optional.

Three decisions from today's commit:

1. **Anonymous by default.** Real identity is opt-in and reversible. Curated avatars,
   generated handles. The intellectual should carry the conversation, not the profile.
2. **Fuzzy by design.** Location is country/region/city plus a coordinate fuzzed to a
   ~15 km grid. It can still light up a map and find a reader-neighbor without ever
   spitting out a house.
3. **Built in public.** Apache 2.0 on day one, ordered SQL migrations, OpenAPI served at
   `/openapi/v1.json`, and a roadmap that says what's shipping and what's not.

What's live in the very first commit: the full API surface (six domains), a healthy web
app to browse it, local `docker compose` infra, Fly.io + Neon + Redis deployment
blueprints, and a smoke test that registers a reader, adds *Dune* to their shelf, starts a
thread in Berlin, reacts to a message, lights up the global map, and resolves a
moderation report. All of it — me included — open source.

Next: affiliate links with public click numbers, the first batch of conversation prompts,
and inviting a few friends to darken the map.
